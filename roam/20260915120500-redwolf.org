* Video Summary: Red Wolf Turns Marine Helicopters Into 200-Mile Shooters

** Overview
The US Marine Corps is seeking greater operational independence for long-range engagements, particularly in preparation for potential conflicts in the Indo-Pacific region. To achieve this, they are adopting the *Redwolf*, a new, cost-effective missile produced by L3 Harris.

** The Redwolf Missile
- *Manufacturer:* L3 Harris
- *Range:* Over 200 nautical miles (approx. 230 miles or 370 km)
- *Cost:* ~$300,000 per unit
- *payloads* up to 25 pounds (11.3 kg)
- *Platform:* Designed for Vertical Takeoff and Landing (VTOL) systems, specifically attack helicopters like the AH-1Z Viper, and potentially drones.
- *Speed & Endurance:* Travels at subsonic speeds and can loiter in the air for up to 60 minutes.
- *Terminology:* Often referred to as a "vehicle" or a "launched effect" because
  it can be outfitted to perform kinetic strikes, electronic warfare, jamming,
  or act as a decoy.

** The PASM Program (Precision Attack Strike Munition)
Redwolf is a key component of PASM (formerly the Long Range Attack Missile
program). This is a Marine aviation effort to bring long-range precision effects
to VTOL platforms.
*** Core Requirements
- *VTOL Family of Systems:* A suite of compatible vehicles and payloads rather than a single-use missile.
- *Aircraft Agnostic:* Uses common interfaces (Universal Armament Interface) to easily move between different launch platforms.
- *Network Enabled:* Capable of receiving in-flight updates and operating beyond line-of-sight using tactical data links like Link 16.
- *Modular Design (WOSA):* Uses Weapons Open System Architecture, allowing the
  military to change payloads and software without a total redesign or vendor
  lock-in.

** Why Range Matters (The Helicopter Problem)
Helicopters are inherently vulnerable because they are slow and lack the altitude to glide-bomb targets from safe distances. In modern contested environments, traditional close-range munitions force helicopters to fly well within the enemy's air defense "kill bubble."
*** Munition Comparisons
- *JAGM (Joint Air-to-Ground Missile):* ~5 miles range, ~$319,000 each.
- *Hellfire (AGM-114):* ~7 miles range, ~$150,000 each.
- *Redwolf:* 200+ nautical miles, ~$300,000 each.
- *Note:* Redwolf provides cruise missile-like standoff ranges from a helicopter
  at a fraction of the cost of traditional long-range options (like the $800k
  JASSM).

** Challenges & Operations
*** Over the Horizon Targeting (The Kill Chain)
Firing a missile 200 nautical miles from a low-flying helicopter means shooting
at targets over the curvature of the Earth that the crew cannot physically see.
- Target tracking and mid-course updates must be fed to the missile by other network assets (satellites, drones, F-35s, ships, or ground sensors).
- Hitting moving targets (like ships) in contested environments means overcoming heavy signal jamming and deception.
*** The Green Wolf Variant
Another variant in the "Wolf" family, designed specifically for non-kinetic
payloads. It can carry jammers, decoys, or microwave weapons to blind enemy
defenses, carving a safe path to the target for the kinetic Redwolf strikes.

** Conclusion
The primary takeaway is a fundamental shift in Marine aviation doctrine. By
outfitting helicopters with low-cost, long-range, network-enabled weapons, the
Marines are transforming them from close-support assets into *standoff network
strike platforms*—a role traditionally reserved for fast jets. This capability
aligns directly with the Marines' Force Design 2030 strategy for Pacific
operations.
